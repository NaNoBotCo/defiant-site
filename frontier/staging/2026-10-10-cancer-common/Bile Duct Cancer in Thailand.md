Bile duct cancer, called **cholangiocarcinoma** by doctors, grows in the thin tubes that carry bile from the liver to the gut. In most of the world it is rare. In northeast Thailand it is one of the commonest cancers there is: registries in Khon Kaen have recorded between 93 and 318 new cases per 100,000 people a year, and Thai surgeons admit 10,000 to 20,000 new cases to surgery annually.[1] The newest drugs for it now run in trials across Thailand, Chiang Mai included.

![[doodle:frontier-bile-duct]]

The drug in this story is **rilvegostomig**, an antibody built by AstraZeneca that releases two brakes on the immune system at once. It is in a Phase 3 trial against the current standard treatment, with recruiting sites in six Thai cities. It is approved nowhere yet.

## Why Thailand

The cause in the northeast is a parasite. *Opisthorchis viverrini*, the small liver fluke, lives in freshwater fish of the carp family. Eaten raw or half-cooked, as in some traditional Isan dishes, the fish passes the fluke to people, and it settles in the bile ducts for years. The World Health Organization's cancer agency classes it as a group 1 carcinogen, the category for things known to cause cancer in people. When Khon Kaen University screened 47,258 people in the northeast, 42.2% carried the fluke.[1]

Rates have been falling in Khon Kaen since the late 1980s, and the regional registry still puts Thai rates at 33.9 per 100,000 in men and 12.9 in women, where the disease is "rare in most parts of the world".[2]

The country answered with a program. **CASCAP**, the Cholangiocarcinoma Screening and Care Program run by Khon Kaen University since 2014, set out to screen 500,000 people at risk by ultrasound and catch the cancer early enough for surgery. By 2016 it had screened 250,000 and found 2,000 cancers.[1] A 2024 analysis of CASCAP's ten years of data across 20 northeastern provinces counted 32 cases per 100,000.[3] Khon Kaen's Faculty of Medicine runs a Cholangiocarcinoma Research Institute, and its Srinagarind Hospital is the referral centre that receives suspected cases from across the region.[4] A separate study in Phu Wiang district, Khon Kaen, is testing a village-level program to change eating habits and stop the cancer before it starts (NCT05321992).

## How immunotherapy reaches this cancer

Your immune system carries brakes. T cells, the white blood cells that kill abnormal cells, wear receptors on their surface that switch them off when touched. These receptors exist to stop the immune system attacking healthy tissue. Tumours learn to press them.

**PD-1** is the best known of these brakes. Drugs that block it, called **checkpoint inhibitors**, release the T cell to attack. Two of them changed bile duct cancer care in the last four years:

- **Durvalumab** added to the standard chemotherapy pair, gemcitabine and cisplatin, raised median survival from 11.5 to 12.8 months in the TOPAZ-1 trial of 685 people. The bigger change was at the tail: about one in four people on durvalumab were alive at two years, against one in ten on chemotherapy alone. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved it on 2 September 2022.[5] The median time before the cancer grew rose from 5.7 to 7.2 months, and tumours shrank in 27% against 19%.[5]
- **Pembrolizumab** with the same chemotherapy raised median survival from 10.9 to 12.7 months in KEYNOTE-966, 1,069 people, and won FDA approval on 31 October 2023.[6]

Outside trials, a study of 1,358 people treated with durvalumab and chemotherapy at 55 hospitals in 12 countries found a median survival of 15.6 months.[7]

**TIGIT** is a second brake receptor on T cells.[8] Rilvegostomig is one antibody with two arms: one grips PD-1, the other grips TIGIT, so both brakes come off on the same cell at the same moment.[8] Its makers describe it as "monovalent" and "Fc-reduced": each arm binds once, and the stem of the antibody is altered so it sets off fewer immune effects of its own.[9] The TIGIT arm came from the Israeli company Compugen, which licensed it to AstraZeneca.[10]

## Early evidence

The bile duct numbers come from **GEMINI-Hepatobiliary**, a Phase 2 study, in its group of 30 people with untreated advanced bile duct cancer. They received rilvegostomig every three weeks for up to two years, with gemcitabine and cisplatin for up to eight cycles.[11]

| Measure | Rilvegostomig + chemotherapy (Phase 2, 30 people) | Durvalumab + chemotherapy (TOPAZ-1, Phase 3) |
|---|---|---|
| Tumour shrank by a third or more | 31% | 27% |
| Disease controlled at 24 weeks | 79.3% | n/a |
| Median time before the cancer grew | 8.3 months | 7.2 months |
| Median survival | 16.8 months (ASCO 2026 update) | 12.8 months |

The TOPAZ-1 column comes from a trial of 685 people; the rilvegostomig column from 30. The figures sit side by side for scale, and a trial comparing the two directly is now recruiting.[5][11]

At the American Society of Clinical Oncology meeting in June 2026, AstraZeneca reported the first survival figure: a median of 16.8 months, against "less than 13 months in historical first-line biliary tract cancer trials". Compugen's own filing adds that "longer follow-up and randomized data from the ongoing Phase 3 trial will ultimately be needed to validate these findings."[10]

Side effects in the 30 people: every person had at least one, and 86.7% had a grade 3 or higher event (severe, on the standard five-step scale) from any cause, chemotherapy included. The commonest treatment-related effect was anaemia, a low red cell count, in 53.3%. Serious events occurred in 40%, and 6.7% were judged due to rilvegostomig. One person in 30 (3.3%) stopped treatment for side effects.[11]

## Why the US has not approved it

Rilvegostomig has not finished a Phase 3 trial in any cancer. A comparison of 30 people against a historical figure is a signal, and drug regulators approve on randomised trials, where the new drug and the standard run side by side in the same hospitals at the same time.

TIGIT drugs carry a history. Roche's tiragolumab, a TIGIT antibody given alongside a separate PD-L1 drug, looked promising in early lung cancer studies and then missed its survival goal in the Phase 3 SKYSCRAPER-01 trial in November 2024, after SKYSCRAPER-06 failed earlier that year.[12] Rilvegostomig differs in design, one molecule on one cell rather than two drugs in the bloodstream, and AstraZeneca is testing whether that difference matters in 12 Phase 3 trials across several cancers.[10] The first bile duct results are expected after 2026.[13]

## The trials with Thai sites

| Trial | Phase, status | What it tests | Thai sites (registry) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ARTEMIDE-Biliary02**, NCT07221253 | Phase 3, recruiting | Rilvegostomig + gemcitabine/cisplatin against durvalumab + gemcitabine/cisplatin, first treatment for advanced disease. 1,100 people worldwide. | Bangkok, **Chiang Mai**, Hat Yai, Khon Kaen, Ubon Ratchathani (Muang), Nakhon Nayok (Ongkharak) |
| **DESTINY-BTC-01**, NCT06467357 | Phase 3, recruiting | Trastuzumab deruxtecan + rilvegostomig against gemcitabine/cisplatin/durvalumab, first treatment, for tumours carrying the HER2 protein. 620 people. | Bangkok, Hat Yai, Khon Kaen, Nakhon Ratchasima, Ongkharak, Si Sa Ket, Muang and Mueang sites |
| **ARTEMIDE-Biliary01**, NCT06109779 | Phase 3, enrolment closed | Rilvegostomig or placebo with chemotherapy after surgery. 760 people. | Hat Yai, Khon Kaen, Chiang Mai, Nakhon Ratchasima, Si Sa Ket, Ubon Ratchathani |
| **BLUESTAR**, NCT05123482 | Phase 1/2a, recruiting | AZD8205, an antibody-drug conjugate, alone or with rilvegostomig, in several cancers including bile duct. | Bangkok, **Chiang Mai** |

In ARTEMIDE-Biliary02, half the people get the current standard, durvalumab and chemotherapy, and half get rilvegostomig and chemotherapy. Neither arm is a placebo, and both doctor and patient know which drug is given. The main measure is survival in people whose tumours carry the PD-L1 marker at 1% or more. The study opened in December 2025 and the registry lists July 2029 for its main result.[14]

**Who can enrol, as the registry states it:** adults 18 and over with bile duct or gallbladder adenocarcinoma confirmed on a tissue sample, which surgery cannot remove or which has spread; no earlier drug treatment for the advanced disease; PD-L1 status measured by the trial's central laboratory; a tumour visible and measurable on CT or MRI; up and about most of the day (ECOG performance status 0 or 1); working blood counts and organ function. Excluded: cancer of the ampulla (where the bile duct meets the gut), any earlier immune checkpoint drug, autoimmune disease needing long-term steroids or immune-suppressing drugs, and active lung inflammation.[14]

DESTINY-BTC-01 is for tumours with the **HER2** protein at the 2+ or 3+ level on a tissue stain. Trastuzumab deruxtecan is an antibody that carries a chemotherapy payload straight to HER2 cells. In the US it has carried an accelerated approval since 5 April 2024 for previously treated HER2 3+ solid tumours of any kind, bile duct among them, where it shrank tumours in 51.4% of people in its main study.[15] This trial asks whether it works as a first treatment, paired with rilvegostomig.[16]

## Evidence, graded

| Treatment | Grade | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Gemcitabine + cisplatin + durvalumab, advanced disease | **Established** | Phase 3, approved in the US and elsewhere since 2022. |
| Gemcitabine + cisplatin + pembrolizumab, advanced disease | **Established** | Phase 3, US approval 2023. |
| Trastuzumab deruxtecan, HER2 3+ tumours after earlier treatment | **Reasonable** | US accelerated approval on response rates across tumour types; confirmatory trials running. |
| Ultrasound screening in fluke regions | **Reasonable** | CASCAP finds cancers early in a high-risk population; a survival benefit from screening is still being measured. |
| Rilvegostomig + chemotherapy, advanced disease | **Experimental** | Phase 2, 30 people, compared with history; Phase 3 recruiting. |
| Rilvegostomig after surgery | **Experimental** | Phase 3 under way, enrolment closed. |

## Prices

Rilvegostomig has no price: it is given only inside trials.

The standard immunotherapy does. In the US, durvalumab was priced at USD 7.976 per milligram by the Medicare price list in July 2023.[17] The bile duct dose with chemotherapy is 1,500 mg every three weeks,[5] so one dose of the drug alone runs 1,500 x 7.976 = about **USD 11,964**, before chemotherapy, infusion and doctor fees.

In Thailand, a Chulalongkorn University researcher quoted an imported anti-PD-1 antibody, the drug class pembrolizumab belongs to, at **219,044 baht per dose** (two vials), about USD 6,618 at 33.10 baht to the dollar, or 3,723,748 baht for a year of 17 doses.[18] The same team is developing a Thai-made anti-PD-1 antibody aimed at tens of thousands of baht a dose. For durvalumab itself, Defiant heard on 10 October 2026 an unconfirmed report of an Indian supplier sourcing it through a licensed Thai pharmacist for about **130,000 baht per dose**, about USD 3,927.[19] A published Thai price for durvalumab is not on record in what this ledger could find.

## What a reader can reach

For a person with advanced bile duct cancer, Thailand offers three things at once: the approved immunotherapy combinations, hospitals in a country that admits 10,000 to 20,000 new cases to surgery a year, and a Phase 3 trial of the next drug with a site in Chiang Mai. Trial places go to people who meet the registry criteria and the site's screening, and the site decides.

Defiant can gather your scans, pathology report and PD-L1 or HER2 results, ask the Thai trial sites whether you would be screened, and arrange the visits in Chiang Mai.
