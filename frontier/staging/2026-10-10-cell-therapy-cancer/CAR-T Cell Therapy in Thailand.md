CAR-T is a cancer drug made from the patient. A hospital draws your blood, separates out the T cells (the white cells that kill infected and abnormal cells), and gives them a new gene. The gene builds a receptor that sticks out from each cell like an antenna, tuned to a protein on the surface of the cancer. The rebuilt cells are grown into the hundreds of millions and dripped back into your vein, where they hunt.

![[doodle:frontier-car-t]]

In the United States the first of these products was approved in 2017, and a course now lists at around half a million dollars before the hospital stay. In Thailand, three medical schools have built the machinery to make CAR-T cells inside their own hospitals, and a fourth quotes about 2 million baht a case. None of it is on a shelf yet. All of it is moving fast.

## What a CAR-T cell is

The name spells the parts. A **chimeric antigen receptor** (CAR) is a receptor stitched together from pieces of different proteins: an antibody tip that recognises the target, a hinge, a section that crosses the cell membrane, and signalling parts inside the cell that switch the T cell on when the tip grabs something. A **T cell** carrying one is a CAR-T cell.

Most CAR-T products aim at **CD19**, a protein on B cells, which is why the approved uses are B-cell cancers: large B-cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, and B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL) in children and young adults. A second group aims at **BCMA**, a protein on the plasma cells of multiple myeloma.

The cells are a single infusion. They multiply inside the body, and in people who respond they can persist for years. The side effects are the cells working too hard: **cytokine release syndrome** (CRS), a fever-and-blood-pressure storm from the immune signals the cells release, and **neurotoxicity**, a spell of confusion or speech trouble. Hospitals treat both with drugs held on the ward for the purpose, which is why CAR-T is given with a stay of a few weeks near the treating unit.

## Thailand's own CAR-T

**Chulalongkorn University and King Chulalongkorn Memorial Hospital (Bangkok).** Chula makes CD19 CAR-T cells in a closed, automated machine (CliniMACS Prodigy) inside the hospital's Cell and Gene Therapy Manufacturing Center, which holds a Thai FDA manufacturing licence. Its paper in *Molecular Therapy Oncology* (June 2026) reports 12 adults with large B-cell lymphoma that had come back or stopped responding, treated between January 2020 and December 2025. Manufacturing worked for 11 of the 12. The median time from drawing blood to infusing cells was 12 days. At three months, 58.3% had responded and 33.3% had no detectable cancer; at one year, 90% were alive. Five had CRS, one of them at grade 3; one had mild neurotoxicity. [1]

The 12 were treated free, under a compassionate-access pilot paid for by a national grant and donors. The paper sets the hospital's own cost at a median of US$111,231 per patient, from blood draw to day 30, US$100,000 of it manufacturing. The authors describe the pilot as the run-up to "a streamlined commercial CD19 CAR T cell service." No patient price is published. [1]

Chula's first CAR-T trial, with Nagoya University, used a different way of delivering the gene (piggyBac, which needs no virus). Its 2023 release reported responses in all of the first five patients. [2] Chula also runs Phase 1 trials of CAR-T aimed at **B7-H3**, a protein on brain tumours and childhood solid tumours, and finished a three-patient trial of CAR-T for lupus. [3]

**Ramathibodi Hospital, Mahidol University (Bangkok).** A paper published in September 2026 follows 11 children and adults with B-cell ALL that had relapsed or resisted treatment, seven of them after a bone-marrow transplant, given locally made CD19 CAR-T from donor cells. All 11 were clear of measurable leukaemia at day 28. At a median follow-up of almost four years, five-year survival stood at 70%. [4] The Ramathibodi team's technology went into **Genepeutic Bio**, a company in Pathum Thani. It finished Phase 1 after Thai FDA approval in 2022, planned Phase 2 for 2026, and intends to file for conditional approval after that. [5]

**Siriraj Hospital, Mahidol University (Bangkok).** A pilot made nine CD19 CAR-T products at the bedside, seven from patients' own cells and two from matched sibling donors. [6]

**Prince of Songkla University (Hat Yai).** In December 2025 the university signed an agreement with the Ministry of Public Health's Department of Medical Services to treat about 12 children with drug-resistant leukaemia, then train government hospital staff. A university spokesperson put its CAR-T at about 2 million baht a case (about US$59,000), against up to 20 million baht abroad. [7]

A commercial CAR-T is made in a central factory, shipped frozen across oceans, and priced to repay a decade of trials. A hospital that makes cells on its own floor cuts the shipping, the factory margin, and the wait. Chula's machine and its lentiviral vector (the disabled virus that carries the gene into the cell) come from Miltenyi Biotec, a German maker of cell-therapy equipment. [1]

The commercial products themselves have not reached Thailand. A 2024 Siriraj paper states the Thai FDA had "not reviewed or approved" Kymriah or Yescarta [6], and a 2025 survey found no CAR-T product reimbursed in Thailand [10].

## The rules that let a hospital make medicine

Thailand's FDA runs a three-part system for **advanced therapy medicinal products** (ATMPs: cell, gene and tissue therapies): authorisation before a product is used, manufacturing standards for the labs, hospitals and medical schools that make them, and registration for commercial sale. Chula's plant was the first certified to make them for research. A sandbox lets a certified medical school supply a partner hospital, inside research. The FDA's secretary-general said in March 2026 that a Thai company in Phase 2 of a cancer gene therapy aims to put the first commercial Thai-made ATMP on the market within 2026. [11]

Stem-cell treatment sits under separate rules. The Medical Council of Thailand's announcement allows stem cells in treatment for blood diseases and eye diseases, with other uses inside approved research. [12]

## What a foreign patient can reach now

**CAR-T itself.** No published source says whether the Chula, Ramathibodi, Siriraj or Prince of Songkla programmes take patients from abroad, and none states a residency rule. The Department of Medical Services describes CAR-T as part of a plan to make Thailand a hub for advanced therapy that draws patients from abroad. [7]

**Trials with Thai sites, recruiting as of 10 October 2026** [3]:

| Trial | What | Phase | Thai sites |
|---|---|---|---|
| NCT07078929 | Chula CD19 CAR-T (CMD63), children 1-18 with relapsed B-ALL | 1 | King Chulalongkorn (recruiting); Siriraj and Maharaj Nakorn Chiang Mai (not yet) |
| NCT07458659 | Chula BCMA CAR-T, myeloma after three or more lines | Early 1 | King Chulalongkorn (not yet recruiting) |
| NCT06091865 | Odronextamab plus chemotherapy, untreated large B-cell lymphoma | 3 | Chulalongkorn, Siriraj, Chiang Mai University, Khon Kaen |
| NCT06149286 | Odronextamab plus lenalidomide, relapsed follicular or marginal zone lymphoma | 3 | Chulalongkorn, Siriraj, Chiang Mai University |
| NCT06549595 | Surovatamig plus rituximab, untreated follicular lymphoma | 3 | Two Bangkok sites, Chiang Mai, Khon Kaen |
| NCT07002203 | Personalised neoantigen vaccine, advanced solid tumours | 1/2 | Bumrungrad, Bangkok |

Odronextamab and surovatamig are **bispecific antibodies**: one molecule with two arms, one gripping the lymphoma cell, the other gripping a T cell, dragging them together. They do off the shelf part of what CAR-T does with custom cells. The registry entries for these three Phase 3 trials list no nationality or residency criterion. The neoantigen vaccine trial lists reading Thai and prior enrolment in the sponsor's earlier project.

**The neoantigen vaccine outside the trial.** Seqker Biosciences, a US company, builds a vaccine from the mutations in one patient's own tumour, working with Thailand's Government Pharmaceutical Organization (GPO) and Chulalongkorn. In September 2026 the GPO's director said two foreign patients, one from Australia and one from the Middle East, had received it at a private hospital in a "semi-research" arrangement, at about 4-7 million baht (US$118,000-206,000) for a year-long course of four doses. She described a plan to propose a long-stay medical visa once three such cases are on record. [13] Applications go through the GPO's online form or LINE account, then assessment by oncologists at partner hospitals. [14]

**Lutetium-177 PSMA.** For prostate cancer that has spread, a radioactive molecule seeks out the PSMA protein on the cancer and irradiates it from inside. Bangkok Hospital's Wattanosoth Cancer Hospital offers it in cycles every six weeks, four to six times, for patients whose PET scan shows high PSMA uptake. The hospital publishes no price. [15]

**Proton therapy.** King Chulalongkorn Memorial Hospital opened Thailand's first proton centre in August 2021 and treated 628 patients in 2022-2023. Proton beams stop inside the tumour instead of passing through, which spares the tissue behind it. A published price for a self-paying patient was not found. [16]

**In Chiang Mai.** Maharaj Nakorn Chiang Mai Hospital, Chiang Mai University's teaching hospital, is a site for the bispecific Phase 3 trials above, for Chula's children's CAR-T trial once it opens there, and for a Phase 3 trial of golidocitinib in peripheral T-cell lymphoma (NCT07234162). [3]

## What comes next

Genepeutic plans its conditional-approval filing after Phase 2. Chula describes its pilot as the step before a commercial service. Prince of Songkla's agreement runs through 2026 into government hospitals. The Thai FDA expects the first registered Thai-made gene therapy within the year. [5][1][7][11] Each would put CAR-T made in Thailand on sale in Thailand. The hospital cost behind it is near US$100,000; the US list price is near $550,000.

## FAQ

### Is CAR-T available in Thailand?
Thai-made CAR-T has been given at Chulalongkorn, Ramathibodi and Siriraj inside research and compassionate-access programmes, and a children's trial is recruiting in Bangkok. Commercial products such as Kymriah and Yescarta were not approved by the Thai FDA as of 2024.

### How much does CAR-T cost in Thailand?
Chula's own cost was a median of US$111,231 per patient to day 30 (2026 paper). Prince of Songkla University quotes about 2 million baht a case (December 2025). Neither is a published price list for international patients.

### How does that compare with the US?
US list prices for the four best-known products ran from $503,580 to $593,533 in 2025, and claims data put the first 30 days of care at $644,000-$692,000.

### Which cancers does it treat?
The approved CD19 products treat B-cell lymphomas and B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukaemia; BCMA products treat multiple myeloma. Thai trials also test CAR-T against brain tumours, childhood solid tumours and lupus.

### Can a foreigner join a Thai cancer trial?
Each trial's sponsor and site decide. The registry entries for the odronextamab and surovatamig Phase 3 trials list no nationality criterion; the Bumrungrad vaccine trial lists reading Thai.

### What is a bispecific antibody?
A single antibody with two arms: one binds the cancer cell, the other binds a T cell, pulling the T cell into contact so it kills. It comes from a vial, so there is no wait for manufacturing.
