Chiang Mai Hospitals for Foreigners: The Working Guide

Chiang Mai has one of the best hospital benches in Southeast Asia for a city its size — a university medical school, half a dozen serious private hospitals, and specialty houses for eyes, nerves, and minds. What it doesn't have is a decent map written for the person who just moved here, or who woke up at 2 a.m. deciding whether tonight is an ER night.

This is that map. Numbers are cross-checked against our own city data and dated; when something changes, tell us and the fix ships the same week.

The two-sentence version

If it's frightening — chest, breath, head, blood — call 1669 (free, nationwide ambulance) or go straight to the nearest private ER and let them sort the rest out. For everything slower than that, the table below tells you who's good at what, and the concierge desk can put a real quote in writing before you commit to anything big.

What a visit costs

Indicative, August 2026, confirmed in writing at intake — the standing policy on every number we publish. A private outpatient visit — doctor, meds, the air-conditioned hour — usually lands between ฿800 and ฿2,500. A private ER visit with imaging commonly runs ฿3,000–15,000. Public hospital outpatient care is a few hundred baht and a longer morning. Admission deposits at private hospitals typically start around ฿20,000–50,000; travel insurance with direct billing makes that line item disappear at the bigger houses.

Which hospital for which problem

  • Anything emergent — nearest 24 h ER, full stop. The emergency numbers card belongs in your phone today.
  • Complex, rare, or oncology — Maharaj Nakorn (Suan Dok) carries the university's specialist bench; Sriphat is the same faculty in a private-wing setting with appointments.
  • Orthopedics, cardiac, checkup packages — Bangkok Hospital Chiang Mai and Chiang Mai Ram both run high-volume programs; this is where the procedure price pages point most often.
  • Eyes — St. Peter Eye Hospital does cataract and retina work all day, every day.
  • A calm, budget-sane admission — McCormick has been doing exactly that since 1893.
  • US veterans — Rajavej's front desk has genuine reps at VA Foreign Medical Program paperwork; the FMP page explains the reimbursement path.

FAQ

Do Chiang Mai hospitals speak English?

At the private hospitals, yes — desks, most physicians, and the billing office. At public hospitals expect Thai first; bring a Thai speaker or hire one of ours for anything with consent forms in it.

Can I just walk in without a referral?

Yes. Thailand runs on walk-ins; even specialist clinics at private hospitals take same-day patients most mornings. Referrals matter mainly inside the public system.

Will my travel insurance work?

The big privates (Bangkok, Ram) direct-bill the major insurers; smaller houses may want payment and give you the paperwork to claim back. Either way the itemized bill you leave with is real — that's part of how you're protected here.

Which hospital is cheapest?

Public beats private by a wide margin, McCormick is the gentlest of the privates, and every elective quote should be confirmed in writing before you book — which is literally the service.
