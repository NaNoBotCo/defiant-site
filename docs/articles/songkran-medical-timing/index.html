The Festival Calendar That Should Schedule Your Surgery

Thai hospitals never close. Emergency rooms run through every holiday on the calendar, and that calendar is one of the richest on earth. But around the ER, the country genuinely reshapes itself several times a year — clinic schedules thin, surgeons travel like everyone else, flights fill, and for three days in April the entire nation holds a water fight. If you're flying in for treatment, the festival calendar isn't trivia. It's scheduling data.

What actually changes on a holiday

  • ERs: nothing. 24 hours, every day of the year — the emergency card works on Songkran morning exactly as it does in November.
  • Outpatient clinics thin out. Specialist OPD sessions shrink or pause on national holidays, and the private-clinic evening sessions many hospital physicians run go quiet. The days around a holiday book heavier as everyone reschedules.
  • Elective surgery slates pause around the big ones — surgeons take holidays too, and post-op reviews need the team present. Plan the operation a comfortable week clear of Songkran, not the Friday before it.
  • Government offices close on every official date — visa extensions, document legalization, and anything at an amphoe (อำเภอ, amphoe — district office) waits until the next business day.
  • Transport and hotels peak at Songkran and New Year. Book both early or arrive after the wave.
  • Buddhist observance days (Makha Bucha, Visakha Bucha, Asalha Bucha) pause alcohol sales nationwide — a footnote for most patients, a planning note for a celebration dinner.

The Songkran rule for fresh wounds

Songkran (สงกรานต์, sǒng-graan — Thai New Year, April 13–15) deserves its own paragraph. It is joyous, national, and wet: for several days, everyone outdoors gets soaked, cheerfully and without exemption. Moat water, ice water, water from strangers on pickup trucks. If you'll be carrying fresh incisions, dressings, or a healing implant in mid-April, plan to be somewhere quiet, or somewhere else — surgical wounds and festival water are a combination no surgeon will bless. Schedule the operation for late April instead and come back for the fun next year, healed.

The good windows

Put the pieces together and the smooth seasons pick themselves:

  • May–June — post-Songkran, pre-rain-peak, cool bills, quiet clinics, soft green mountains.
  • September–November — the year's sweet spot: rains easing, air clean, full clinic schedules, and Yi Peng's lanterns as the reward at the end of recovery. Book November lodging early.
  • December–January works with light planning around New Year's week.
  • February–April carries two calendars at once — the holiday wave and burning season. It's all manageable; it's just the season where planning earns its keep.

FAQ

When is Songkran in Thailand?

April 13–15, every year — the official national holiday. Celebrations in Chiang Mai often stretch a few days longer around the moat.

Are hospitals in Thailand open during Songkran?

Emergency rooms, fully — 24 hours, as always. Outpatient clinics and elective surgery schedules thin around the holiday and refill quickly after.

What is the best time of year for medical treatment in Thailand?

May–June and September–November combine full clinic schedules, clean air, and easy logistics. The engine behind this page keeps the holiday table current, so check it against your dates.

Do Thai holidays affect visa services?

Yes — immigration and government offices close on every official holiday. Extensions and paperwork belong on the business days around them.
