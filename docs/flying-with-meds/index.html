Flying with your meds — Thailand's airport rules, both directions

Nobody standing at the baggage carousel with a bag full of prescriptions should be guessing. The rules exist, they are published in English by Thailand's own Food and Drug Administration, and for most travelers they are easier than the forums make them sound. This page is those rules, read from the FDA's own traveler guidance — inbound, version 5, dated 21 September 2024 and outbound, version 4, dated 29 January 2021 — with the source attached to every claim. Rules change; the FDA itself says to re-check the permit portal before each trip.

Not medical advice and not legal advice — Defiant holds no view on what anyone should take. This page is about paperwork and borders: which lane your bottle goes through and what document it needs.

Arriving in Thailand: the three lanes

Which lane you are in depends on what Thai law calls your medicine — not what your home country calls it. The FDA runs a check-the-drug search where you type the generic (molecule) name and get the authority's own answer about your own bottle. Use it before you book anything.

Lane 1 — ordinary prescription medicine: bring it, with papers

Most medication is in this lane: blood-pressure pills, statins, thyroid, metformin, insulin, hormones, antidepressants that aren't controlled. The FDA's guidance treats medicine as personal belongings — green channel, nothing to declare — when it is for your own use, no more than a 30-day supply, and accompanied by a prescription or doctor's certificate in your name. Keep it in the original, labeled packaging and keep the prescription with you for the whole stay.

Inference — the published traveler guidance speaks to the 30-day lane and is silent on longer supplies of non-controlled medicine. If you're moving here with six months of refills, the clean plays are: carry full documentation for all of it, or land with 30 days and have a Thai doctor re-prescribe — most common molecules are on Thai shelves, usually cheaper. Our estradiol page shows how that translation works in practice: bring the molecule name, not the US brand name.

Lane 2 — psychotropics (Schedule II, III, IV): papers to 30 days, permit past it

This is the lane people fall into without knowing it: alprazolam, diazepam, clonazepam, lorazepam, zolpidem, zopiclone, temazepam, midazolam, methylphenidate, phentermine, phenobarbital — even pseudoephedrine. The FDA's stated rule has two tiers:

  • Up to a 30-day supply — no permit. Carry a certificate or prescription from the prescribing physician with the details listed below, and you're legal.
  • A 31-to-90-day supply — you need a Form IC-2 permit from the Thai FDA, applied for at least 15 days before you land.

Lane 3 — narcotics (Schedule II, III): permit always, red channel

Opioid and opioid-adjacent medication — codeine, morphine, oxycodone, hydrocodone, fentanyl, methadone, pethidine, tapentadol, diphenoxylate — needs the Form IC-2 permit no matter the quantity, up to a maximum 90-day supply, under the Ministerial Regulation that took effect 21 September 2024. On arrival you go through the red channel and present the medication, permit, and medical documents together.

The never lane

Some substances cannot enter Thailand with any paperwork, because Thai law classes them as having no medical use here. The FDA names amphetamine and dextroamphetamine — that is Adderall and its relatives, prescribed daily by US doctors and flatly non-importable here — plus Schedule I psychotropics such as dronabinol (THC), GHB, and psilocin. If your treatment depends on one of these, the FDA's own advice is to talk to your doctor about alternatives before you travel; a Thai psychiatrist can prescribe methylphenidate, which is legal here under Lane 2 rules. Don't test this lane. Thai drug law is not a negotiation.

How the Form IC-2 permit actually works

The FDA's stated process, from the guidance document:

  • Apply online at permitfortraveler.fda.moph.go.th at least 15 days before arrival; processing takes about 3 working days once your application is complete.
  • The FDA's own words: use a PC or laptop with Chrome — the application cannot be completed on a phone.
  • The permit arrives by email. Print it or show the file on your phone; you do not mail original documents anywhere — keep them with you while traveling.
  • At the airport, present medication + permit + medical documents at the red channel.
  • If you obtained an import permit on the way in, the FDA states you do not need a separate export permit to carry the same medicine home.

What the prescription has to say

For every controlled lane, the FDA requires a prescription or signed medical document containing, exactly:

  • Your name and address
  • The diagnosed condition
  • The medication names, strengths, instructions for use, and the reason prescribed
  • The dosage and total amount prescribed
  • The prescribing physician's name, address, and license number

A pharmacy label alone doesn't carry all five. Ask your doctor for a travel letter that does — it costs a phone call at home and saves an argument at a customs desk.

Leaving Thailand with Thai-prescribed medicine

The return trip has its own rules, and for medical tourists this is the half that matters — you came for treatment, and you're flying home with the medication that follows it.

  • Ordinary Thai prescriptions — keep the hospital or pharmacy label and the receipt. The Thai side is easy; your destination country's law is the one that bites. The US lets residents bring in personal-use medication with a prescription; check your own country's rules before assuming.
  • Psychotropics prescribed here (the Lane 2 list above) — up to a 30-day supply with the prescription, no Thai permit required.
  • Narcotics prescribed here (Category 2 — the opioid list) — a Form OC-2 permit from the Thai FDA, applied for on the same portal at least two weeks before departure, up to a 90-day supply. The FDA states the OC-2 also covers whatever remains when you return to Thailand — built for exactly the treated-here, home-for-a-while, back-for-follow-up rhythm our DTV guide describes.

Bought legally here ≠ flies legally

Chiang Mai will sell you cannabis products in a storefront and kratom at a market stall. None of it crosses the border. The FDA's outbound guidance states that import and export of cannabis-made medicinal products is prohibited, and whatever your destination country thinks of either substance applies on top. The rule of thumb that never fails: anything you'd hesitate to hand a customs officer with a smile, don't pack.

The security belt is a different checkpoint

Customs cares what the substance is; security screening cares that it's a liquid. Thai airports follow the standard 100ml carry-on rule, with a stated exemption: medication in liquid, gel, or aerosol form over 100ml is allowed when accompanied by documentation bearing your name — a doctor's note or the prescription label — and it gets a secondary check at the belt.

Inference — temperature-sensitive medication like insulin belongs in your carry-on regardless: checked baggage holds freeze, bake, and disappear. A cool-bag with your name on the prescription has boarded thousands of flights. Flying with a refrigerated biologic — Ilumya, Cosentyx, Dupixent and their class — has its own per-drug clock; the hour-by-hour table is on the biologics page.

FAQ

Do I need any permit for regular medication like blood-pressure pills?

No. Non-controlled medication up to a 30-day supply travels as personal belongings — original packaging plus a prescription or doctor's letter in your name. Green channel.

Is Adderall really banned in Thailand?

For import, yes. The Thai FDA lists amphetamine and dextroamphetamine as prohibited with no medical use in Thailand — no permit exists for them. Methylphenidate is legal here as a Schedule II psychotropic: importable to 30 days with a prescription, and prescribable by a Thai psychiatrist.

What about my sleeping pills or anxiety medication?

Most (zolpidem, alprazolam, diazepam, and relatives) are Thai-scheduled psychotropics: up to a 30-day supply needs only the prescription; 31 to 90 days needs the Form IC-2 permit filed at least 15 days before you fly.

How do I check my specific medicine?

Type the generic name into the Thai FDA's own check-the-drug tool. That answer outranks this page, your pharmacist, and every forum thread.

Can I take medicine a Thai hospital prescribed back home with me?

Yes — ordinary prescriptions travel with their label and receipt, Thai-scheduled psychotropics to 30 days with the prescription, and opioid-class medication with a Form OC-2 permit applied for two weeks before departure. Then your home country's import rules apply at the other end.
