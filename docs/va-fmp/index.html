Your VA benefits work in Thailand

Veterans: the VA will pay for care you get in Thailand. Most vets over here don't know that, or tried once, got buried in paperwork, and gave up. It's real, it's called the Foreign Medical Program (FMP), and the whole game is knowing the two rules and picking the right hospital.

Not legal or VA advice — Defiant is a concierge, not the VA. Every figure below is from public VA sources (linked at the bottom); your benefits authorization letter is the final word on what's covered. We help you navigate it; we don't decide it.

The two rules that trip everyone up

1. Service-connected only. FMP reimburses care for the conditions the VA has rated as service-connected — plus any condition that aggravates one. That knee the VA rated at 30%? Covered. The flu you caught last week? Not. This is the #1 misunderstanding, and it's the whole boundary of the program. 2. You must be registered before it pays. FMP isn't automatic with your disability rating — you enroll, and the VA sends you a benefits authorization letter listing exactly which conditions they'll cover abroad. That letter is your golden ticket; bring it to every appointment.

How it actually pays

Thailand is reimbursement-based — there's no VA clinic here (the Philippines is the one exception in the region, with a VA outpatient clinic in Manila). So the model is:

  • You get treated for a covered condition, pay or arrange billing, and a claim

    • goes to the VA (VA Form 10-7959f-2, now also fileable online).

  • The VA reimburses — these days by direct deposit — for covered services,

    • FDA-approved medications, and durable medical equipment.

  • Inference — a hospital experienced with FMP will hand you an **itemized bill

    • in English with the diagnosis and codes the VA needs, keep proper records, and sometimes coordinate billing directly. A hospital that isn't experienced will hand you a Thai-language receipt that turns your claim into a months-long fight. That difference is the entire reason this page exists.**

The real part

  • FMP won't cover your non-service-connected care. But here's the Defiant

    • angle: in Thailand you're paying cash Thai prices for that — a fraction of US cost — so the stuff FMP doesn't cover still doesn't bankrupt you. FMP for the service-connected, Thai cash prices for the rest. That combined math is why so many vets retire here.

  • We are not the VA and can't change a rating or a decision. What we do:

    • help you register, point you at FMP-ready hospitals, send an interpreter, and keep your paperwork clean so claims actually get paid.
